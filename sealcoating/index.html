Sealcoating That Buys Your Asphalt Years, Not Months.

A protective coat over sound asphalt — it slows the UV, water, and fuel damage that turns a grey lot into a resurfacing bill, and it gives new striping a clean black field to sit on.

The Work

Prepped Properly, Coated Evenly.

Asphalt fails from the top down. Sun oxidizes the binder until the surface goes grey and brittle, rain works into the cracks that opens up, and fuel and oil soften whatever is left. Sealcoating puts a sacrificial layer between all of that and the pavement you actually paid for.

The work is only worth doing on a surface that is sound underneath, so we clean the lot, fill the cracks, and handle failed areas first. Sealing over a lot that needs repair just hides the problem for a season and costs you the price of the seal.

What’s Included

What a sealcoating project includes

Cleaning and prep

The lot is swept and blown clean, and oil-spotted areas are treated — sealer will not bond to dirt or to a patch of soaked-in motor oil.

Crack filling first

Open cracks are filled before any sealer goes down. Sealcoat is a surface coat, not a filler, and it will telegraph every crack you leave under it.

Repairs where the surface has failed

Potholes and failed sections are cut out and rebuilt first, so the finished lot is uniform instead of a smooth coat over soft spots.

Even application, clean edges

Sealer applied at an even rate with cut-in edges along curbs, walks, and concrete aprons — no overspray on the sidewalk, no thin streaks through the drive lanes.

Restriping over the fresh coat

A sealed lot has to be restriped, and that is the best part: new paint on a fresh black surface reads sharper than it ever will on faded grey asphalt.

How often should a commercial lot be sealcoated?

It depends on traffic, drainage, and sun exposure, but most Houston-area commercial lots benefit from sealcoating every two to four years. The honest test is the color: asphalt that has gone light grey and feels rough has already lost its binder at the surface.

How long is the lot out of service?

Sealer needs to cure before traffic, so a lot is typically closed overnight or through a weekend, and larger properties are phased so only part of the lot closes at a time. Striping goes down once the coat has cured. We give you the schedule before we start.

Is sealcoating the same as resurfacing?

No, and it is worth being clear about the difference. Sealcoating is a protective coat over pavement that is still structurally sound. Resurfacing lays new asphalt over a failing surface. Sealcoating is what keeps you from needing the second one as soon.

Will sealcoating fix cracks and potholes?

It will not. Sealer is roughly the thickness of paint — it covers, it does not fill. Cracks get filled and failed areas get repaired first, and anything left underneath will show through and reopen. Any contractor willing to seal straight over damage is selling you a season.

Do you sealcoat concrete?

Sealcoating is an asphalt product. Concrete lots are maintained differently — joint sealing, repair, and striping. Send the property address through the quote form and we will tell you which one you actually have.
