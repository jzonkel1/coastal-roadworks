The Work

Wheel Stops & Parking Lot Signage, Done Properly.

Paint organizes a lot, but hardware defends it. Wheel stops keep vehicles off sidewalks, out of landscaping, and away from your storefront glass. Signs put your rules in writing: who parks where, which lanes stay clear, and what happens to vehicles that ignore both.

We install concrete and rubber wheel stops pinned properly to the pavement, and post signs — reserved, accessible, fire lane, tow-away, and property-specific — mounted straight, at the right height, where drivers actually look.

Concrete or rubber wheel stops — which should I pick?

Concrete is the standard for durability and cost. Rubber is friendlier to snow plows and car bumpers and installs fast. We will recommend one based on your traffic and budget rather than defaulting to either.

Do you supply the signs or do I?

Either works. We can source standard traffic, reserved, and accessible signage, or install signs your property already has on hand.

Can wheel stops be installed on an occupied lot?

Yes. Installation is quick per stall, and we phase the work so only a small section of parking is blocked at a time.

Do accessible stalls need signs and paint?

Accessible stalls are generally expected to have both a posted sign and painted markings. We handle the two together so the signage and the paint match.
